How SkillMesh works

1. Two contracts, one relationship

CertifierOracle issues and stores certifications. HiringBoard is a completely separate contract that never stores or judges skill evidence itself — it simply calls oracle.view().is_certified(candidate, skill) before accepting an application. This is genuine contract composability: the Board trusts the Oracle's on-chain answer instead of re-implementing verification.

2. Certification is a judgment, not a checkbox

You submit specific evidence of competency. A GenLayer validator leader judges whether it demonstrates real skill; other validators independently re-judge the same evidence, and the network only records a verdict once they agree. Vague claims fail; specific, demonstrable work passes.

3. Certifications are reusable

Once issued, a certification lives permanently on the Oracle. Any number of other apps — not just this Job Board — can check it with a free, walletless read call. You prove a skill once and reuse that proof everywhere.

4. No staking, no fees

There's nothing to stake or pay beyond the small GenLayer network fee for your own transaction. Applying to a job costs nothing extra even if you're rejected.

5. A proof link is real, checkable evidence

Anyone can type confident-sounding text, so evidence text alone is weak proof. When you attach a proof link (a repo, a live demo, a published article), validators fetch that page themselves — independently, not from what you typed — and weigh it heavily. A submission with no link is judged as self-reported only and held to a stricter bar.

6. A job can need more than one hire

Every job posting sets how many positions it needs. The employer can mark multiple certified applicants as hired, one at a time, until every position is filled — only then does the job close to new applications.

7. My Profile

Connect a wallet and open My Profile to see everything tied to your address in one place: your certificates, the jobs you've posted, the jobs you've applied to (and their status), and the jobs you've been hired into.
